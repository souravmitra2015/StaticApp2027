                                    40 IVEX PROSPERITY THROUGH PEOPLESMED Case Study: Improving Efficiency in Fibers Production Black-to-White and White-to-Black Transitions at our Polyester Fibers site in ThailandOur Fibers site in Thailand, Indorama Polyester Industries Nakhon Pathom (IPINPT), recently completed an improvement project: Reducing Downtime for Product Changeovers: Black-to-White and White-to-Black Transitions. The project aimed to boost efficiency in polyester fiber production by minimizing the downtime required to switch between black and white fibers.In 2023%u20132024, the average downtime per transition was 6,070 minutes, resulting in substantial productivity and financial losses. Key factors contributing to this inefficiency included limited cleaning equipment, extensive cleaning areas, and the absence of effective Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). The SMED project set out to reduce changeover times, achieving significant cost savings and enhanced production efficiency.The SMED methodology was applied to identify and transform internal elements of the changeover process into external ones, thereby reducing overall downtime. The steps included:1. Separation of External Elements: Tasks like spinning finish cleaning, can production cleaning, and roller brush holder cleaning were separated into external work, reducing the total time by 1,230 minutes.2. Conversion and Removal: Increasing manpower from two to four people for cleaning the inlet roller and draw bath reduced time by 280 minutes.3. Streamlining Processes: Streamlining further reduced the baler cleaning time from 788 minutes to 480 minutes and the dryer cleaning time from 720 minutes to 352 minutes.The SMED project successfully reduced the downtime for product changeovers from an average of 6,070 minutes to a targeted 2,880 minutes (achieving a reduction of 2,186 minutes, bringing the total downtime to 3,884 minutes). This led to significant cost savings of up to 46,063.6 USD per year. By systematically addressing inefficiencies through SMED, the polyester fiber production process became more efficient, yielding substantial productivity improvements and financial benefits.
